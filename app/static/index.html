How far to trust this issue

By the FACT editors
  • It judges writing style, not truth. A true story told like a joke can come out fake.
  • It learned from Reddit posts from 2008 to 2019. New names and topics can confuse it.
  • The fake and real labels come from the subreddit, not from fact-checkers.
  • It reads English headlines only.
  • The photo check learned one generator. It often calls polished real photos from other publishers generated.

Four checks no model does for you

  1. Find the original. Who published it first, and are they real?
  2. Search the claim on a fact-checking site such as Demaskuok or EUvsDisinfo.
  3. Reverse-search the photo to see where and when it first appeared.
  4. Check the date. Old stories often come back as new ones.

This prototype ranks posts for a person to check. It does not decide what is true.

How this issue was made

Two models read every submission. Neither one knows what is true.

Every headline in FACT is read by DistilBERT, a language model that was fine-tuned on 564,000 Reddit posts from the Fakeddit dataset. Each post was labelled by the subreddit it came from, from r/TheOnion to r/usnews, so the model learned how fake and real posts tend to be written.

Photos go to CLIP, an image model that turns a picture into 768 numbers, and a small classifier trained on 10,000 news photos, half of them made by Midjourney. It is very good at that one generator and weaker on others.

Both verdicts sit side by side on page 3, because a true-sounding story can carry a made-up picture. Neither model knows what is true. They know what fake news on Reddit used to look like, which is why a person should always make the final call.

How to read FACT
