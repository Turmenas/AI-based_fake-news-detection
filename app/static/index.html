How far to trust this issue

By the FACT editors
  • It judges how a headline is written. A true story told like a joke can come out fake.
  • It learned from Reddit posts from 2008 to 2019. New names and topics can confuse it.
  • Each post was labelled fake or real by the subreddit it came from. No fact-checker looked at them.
  • It reads English headlines only.
  • The photo check learned one generator. It often mistakes polished real photos from other publishers for AI ones.

Four checks no model does for you

  1. Find the original. Who published it first, and are they real?
  2. Search the claim on a fact-checking site such as Demaskuok or EUvsDisinfo.
  3. Reverse-search the photo to see where and when it first appeared.
  4. Check the date. Old stories often come back as new ones.

The verdict on page 3 describes how the headline is written. Whether the story is true is still for you to check.

How this issue was made

A language model reads the headline and an image model checks the photo.

Every headline in FACT is read by DistilBERT, a language model that was fine-tuned on 564,000 Reddit posts from the Fakeddit dataset. Each post was labelled by its subreddit, so the model learned how fake and real posts tend to be written.

Photos go to CLIP and a small classifier trained on 10,000 news photos, half of them made by Midjourney. It knows that one generator well and others less.

Neither model checks facts. The headline model knows what fake posts on Reddit looked like up to 2019, and the photo model knows what Midjourney images look like. The checks on page 8 are still up to the reader.
